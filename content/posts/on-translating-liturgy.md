A liturgical text is not read; it is *done*. That single fact changes almost
every decision a translator makes.

## Accuracy, rhythm, recitability

A philosophical treatise can tolerate an awkward but precise rendering. A
sādhana cannot — it must scan, breathe and land well when recited aloud,
day after day, often in a group. Three pressures pull at every line:

- **Accuracy** — the meaning of the Tibetan, which is not negotiable.
- **Rhythm** — Tibetan verse is metrical; English and Polish want their own
  music, not a photocopy of the Tibetan metre.
- **Recitability** — no tongue-twisters, no lines that run out of breath.

> The test of a liturgical translation is not the desk but the shrine room.
