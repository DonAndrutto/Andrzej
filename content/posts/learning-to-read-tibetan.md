Many practitioners recite Tibetan daily — from transliteration. The script
itself, with its stacked letters and silent consonants, can seem like a wall.
It isn't. It is a beautifully systematic alphabet, and a few weeks of steady
practice are enough to begin sounding out real texts.

[Reading & Writing Tibetan](https://donandrutto.github.io/TibReading/) is an
interactive primer built to get you over that wall.

## Based on a classic manual

The primer follows Gen Dawa Tshering's *Manual of Colloquial Tibetan*, a
well-loved introduction to the language. The app takes its progression —
letters, vowel signs, then the combinations that make Tibetan spelling feel
opaque — and makes each step interactive.

## Why learn the script

Reading the script changes a practitioner's relationship with the texts:

1. **Transliteration is a crutch** — useful at first, but it hides the
   structure of the language and often distorts pronunciation.
2. **Editions differ** — being able to check the Tibetan, even slowly,
   resolves questions that no transliteration can.
3. **The script is part of the tradition** — the letters themselves are
   treated with respect in Tibetan Buddhist culture, and knowing them opens
   doors everywhere: pecha pages, thangka inscriptions, prayer wheels.

Like the other study aids on this site, the primer is free. Start with the
